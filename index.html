Product design, from object to campaign

Most product shoots start with a product. These started with nothing — the object is designed first, locked as a single master reference, and every later frame is built around that lock. It matters because the usual failure of AI product work is drift: eight beautiful images of eight subtly different bottles. Lock the object once and the macro, the range shot and the flat lay are all provably the same thing.

The same method, a different temperature — a luxury serum built as a museum object rather than a cosmetic.

VOID and LUMA are invented brands, designed and shot for this portfolio. No client commissioned either one. The method is the deliverable: on a real job the object is the client's, and the same lock keeps it identical across every frame they order — including the ones they order six months later.

Client work

Character design & consistency

A five-character cast plus four reference sheets for a wellness app. The point of a reference sheet is that the character survives being drawn again — same face, same proportions, same line weight, in every pose, months later, by anyone on the team.

One cast, one house style, five people who still read as individuals. Consistency across a set is the whole job.

Concept work

Team headshots that match

A company About page usually collects six photos taken in six different places — one from a wedding, one cropped out of a group shot, one lit by a ceiling bulb. It reads as six freelancers rather than one company. This is the same six people rebuilt into a single set: one background, one lighting direction, one crop height. Everyone sent an ordinary photo; nobody left their desk.

The six-person set is made of invented people, generated for this portfolio. The single before/after above is a real person, used with his permission. On a real job the photos belong to the client's own team, are used with each person's consent, and are deleted on delivery.
